# Privet

**Scientific name:** Ligustrum vulgare  
**Swedish:** Liguster  
**Polish:** Ligustr pospolity

## Basic Information

Privet is one of the most widely used hedging plants in Scandinavia — fast-growing, dense, and extraordinarily tolerant of pruning. A semi-evergreen shrub, it keeps most of its small glossy leaves through mild winters and regenerates quickly in spring. In the back garden it forms the side border hedges, providing privacy, wind shelter, and a clean green backdrop for the rest of the planting.

### Characteristics
- **Type:** Semi-evergreen shrub / hedge
- **Height:** 2–4 meters (unpruned); maintained to any height by clipping
- **Spread:** 1–2 meters per plant; forms a continuous hedge when planted closely
- **Growth rate:** Fast (30–50 cm per year)
- **Flowers:** Small creamy-white panicles in June–July, sweetly scented
- **Berries:** Small black berries in autumn (mildly toxic — do not eat)
- **Foliage:** Small (2–4 cm), oval, glossy dark green leaves in opposite pairs
- **Lifespan:** 30–40+ years

### Growing Conditions
- **Light:** Full sun to deep shade (one of the most shade-tolerant hedge plants)
- **Soil:** Extremely adaptable; tolerates clay, sand, chalk, and poor soil
- **pH:** 5.5–8.0
- **Hardiness:** Zone 4–7 (fully hardy in the Stockholm area)

## Location Notes

Privet is well-suited to the Stockholm climate:
- Fully winter-hardy; no protection required
- Semi-evergreen — retains leaves in mild winters, drops them in harsh ones but recovers quickly
- Tolerates the partial shade along the side borders of the back garden
- Excellent wind and noise buffer for the garden
- Provides nesting habitat for garden birds
- The berries attract thrushes and blackbirds in autumn

## Monthly Care Calendar

### January
- **Tasks:** None required
- **Watch for:** Plant may be semi-dormant; leaves present or dropped depending on winter severity

### February
- **Tasks:** None required
- **Watch for:** New buds may begin to swell in late February during mild spells

### March
- **Tasks:**
  - Inspect hedge for winter damage or dead branches
  - Remove any completely dead stems at the base
- **Watch for:** Fresh leaf growth emerging from bare sections

### April
- **Tasks:**
  - Apply balanced slow-release fertilizer along the hedge base
  - Mulch along the base with täckbark brun (5–8 cm layer)
- **Watch for:** Vigorous new growth starting; hedge filling in

### May
- **Tasks:**
  - Water newly planted sections during dry spells
  - Established hedges generally don't need watering
- **Watch for:** Rapid growth — hedge may need first trim by late May

### June
- **Tasks:**
  - **First trim of the season** (see Pruning Guide)
  - Trim after flowering if you want to keep the blooms; before flowering if a tidy line matters more
- **Watch for:** Small white flower panicles appearing — fragrant but not showy

### July
- **Tasks:**
  - Water during prolonged dry spells (especially in sandy soil)
  - Light shaping if needed
- **Watch for:** Berries beginning to form after flowering

### August
- **Tasks:**
  - **Second trim of the season** — gives a clean shape going into autumn
- **Watch for:** Growth rate slowing down; hedge holding its shape better

### September
- **Tasks:**
  - No trimming after early September — late cuts produce soft growth vulnerable to frost
- **Watch for:** Black berries ripening; birds feeding on them

### October
- **Tasks:**
  - Top up mulch along the base if needed (5–8 cm)
  - Deep water before ground freezes if autumn has been very dry
- **Watch for:** Leaves may start to drop in cold autumns

### November
- **Tasks:** None required
- **Watch for:** Semi-evergreen behaviour — some leaves persist, others drop

### December
- **Tasks:** None required
- **Watch for:** Fully dormant; structural shape visible

## Pruning Guide

### Why Privet Is Easy to Prune
Privet is one of the most forgiving hedging plants. It responds to pruning by branching densely, so even hard cuts result in thick regrowth. You can clip it at almost any time during the growing season without harm.

### When to Trim
- **Main trims:** Twice per year — June and August
- **Light touch-ups:** Any time from May to early September
- **Avoid:** Late September onward — new growth won't harden before frost

### Hedge Trimming Method

1. **Use a taut string line** or follow the existing hedge profile for a straight top
2. **Trim the sides first**, working from bottom to top
3. **Trim the top last**
4. **Taper slightly** — the hedge should be very slightly wider at the base than the top so light reaches the lower leaves
5. **Clear up clippings** — they can smother the base if left in a thick layer

### Renovation Pruning
If the hedge has become bare at the base or overgrown:
- Cut back hard to 30–45 cm from ground level in late winter (February–March)
- Privet regenerates vigorously from old wood
- The hedge will look bare for one season but fill in by the following year
- Feed well after hard pruning

## Seasonal Considerations

### Before Spring (January–March)
- No action needed for established hedges
- Good time to plan renovation pruning if the hedge is patchy

### Spring–Summer (April–August)
- Peak growing season — two trims keep the hedge neat
- Feed once in April; the hedge is otherwise self-sufficient
- Flowers in June attract pollinators

### Autumn (September–November)
- No trimming; let the hedge harden for winter
- Berries provide food for birds
- Top up mulch if needed

### Winter (December–February)
- Fully hardy — no protection needed in Stockholm
- May lose some or all leaves in a harsh winter; this is normal
- Recovers quickly in spring

## Common Problems in Swedish Climate

### Pests
- **Privet thrips:** Can cause silvery discolouration on leaves; usually minor
- **Aphids:** Occasionally on new growth in spring; rarely significant on hedges
- **Privet hawk-moth caterpillars:** Large green caterpillars; interesting to spot but rarely cause serious damage
- **Generally very pest-resistant**

### Diseases
- **Honey fungus (Armillaria):** The most serious threat — causes dieback and death; remove affected plants and do not replant privet in the same spot
- **Leaf spot:** Brown spots on leaves in wet summers; cosmetic only
- **Powdery mildew:** White coating on leaves in dry conditions; improve air circulation

### Environmental Issues
- **Bare at the base:** Usually from years of trimming the top wider than the bottom — light can't reach lower branches. Fix with tapered trimming or renovation pruning.
- **Leaf drop in winter:** Normal for Ligustrum vulgare in cold climates; not a sign of disease
- **Slow recovery after hard frost:** Give it time; privet almost always bounces back by June

## Tips for Success

1. **Trim twice a year** — June and August keeps the hedge dense and tidy without much effort

2. **Taper the profile** — slightly narrower at the top than the base ensures light reaches lower branches and prevents bare legs

3. **Don't worry about winter leaf drop** — it's normal for wild privet in Stockholm; the hedge fills in fast each spring

4. **Mulch the base** — täckbark brun along the hedge line suppresses weeds and retains moisture

5. **Feed once in spring** — a single application of balanced fertilizer in April is all it needs

6. **Leave berries for birds** — the black autumn berries are mildly toxic to humans but excellent food for thrushes and blackbirds

7. **Renovation is always an option** — even a neglected privet hedge can be cut back hard and will regrow densely within a season or two
